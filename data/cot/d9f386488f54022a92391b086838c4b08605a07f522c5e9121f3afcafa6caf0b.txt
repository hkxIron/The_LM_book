   \[
   P = 4 \times \text{side length}
   \]

   \[
   P = 4 \times 15 = 60 \text{ miles}

   \[
   \text{Swimming speed} = \frac{1 \text{ mile}}{20 \text{ minutes}} = \frac{1 \text{ mile}}{\frac{20}{60} \text{ hours}} = \frac{1 \text{ mile}}{\frac{1}{3} \text{ hours}} = 3 \text{ miles per hour}

3. **Calculate Jake's rowing speed.**
   It is given that Jake can row at twice the speed he can swim. Therefore, his rowing speed is:
   \[
   \text{Rowing speed} = 2 \times \text{Swimming speed} = 2 \times 3 = 6 \text{ miles per hour}
   \]

4. **Calculate the time it takes to row around the lake.**
   Now that we know the perimeter of the lake is 60 miles and Jake's rowing speed is 6 miles per hour, we can find out how long it takes to row around the lake using the formula:
   \[
   \text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}}
   \]
   Substituting the values we have:
   \[
   \text{Time} = \frac{60 \text{ miles}}{6 \text{ miles per hour}} = 10 \text{ hours}
   \]
