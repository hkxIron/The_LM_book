<reasoning>
Let's denote the amounts of money each person has using variables:

- Let \( K \) be the amount of money Kent has.
- Let \( B \) be the amount of money Brooke has.
- Let \( Br \) be the amount of money Brittany has.
- Let \( A \) be the amount of money Alison has.

From the problem, we know the following:

1. Kent has $1,000, so \( K = 1000 \).
2. Brooke has twice as much money as Kent, so we can express Brooke's money as:
   \[
   B = 2K
   \]
   Substituting \( K = 1000 \):
   \[
   B = 2 \times 1000 = 2000
   \]

3. Brittany has 4 times as much money as Brooke, which gives us:
   \[
   Br = 4B
   \]
   Substituting \( B = 2000 \):
   \[
   Br = 4 \times 2000 = 8000
   \]

4. Alison has half as much money as Brittany, so we can express Alison's money as:
   \[
   A = \frac{1}{2}Br
   \]
   Substituting \( Br = 8000 \):
   \[
   A = \frac{1}{2} \times 8000 = 4000
   \]

Now we have found how much money Alison has.

Therefore, Alison has $4,000.
