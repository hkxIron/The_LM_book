<reasoning>
First, let's calculate how much Tim paid for his doctor's visit.

1. Tim's doctor's visit costs $300. The insurance covers 75% of this amount.
2. To find out how much the insurance covers, we calculate 75% of $300:
   \[
   \text{Insurance coverage} = 0.75 \times 300 = 225
   \]
3. Now, we subtract the insurance coverage from the total cost to find out how much Tim had to pay out of pocket:
   \[
   \text{Out-of-pocket cost for doctor's visit} = 300 - 225 = 75
   \]

Next, let's calculate how much Tim paid for his cat's visit.

4. The cat's visit costs $120 and the pet insurance covers $60.
5. To find out how much Tim paid for the cat's visit, we subtract the insurance coverage from the total cost:
   \[
   \text{Out-of-pocket cost for cat's visit} = 120 - 60 = 60
   \]

Now, we can find the total amount Tim paid for both visits.

6. We add the out-of-pocket costs for both the doctor's visit and the cat's visit:
   \[
   \text{Total amount paid} = 75 + 60 = 135
   \]

Thus, Tim paid a total of $135 for both his and his cat's visits.
</reasoning>
<answer>135</answer>
