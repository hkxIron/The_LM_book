Let's denote the amount of money Jane has as \( J \). Since Jean has three times as much money as Jane, we can express the amount of money Jean has as \( 3J \).

Now, we know from the problem that together they have a total of $76. Therefore, we can write the equation:

\[
J + 3J = 76
\]

Combining the terms on the left side, we have:

\[
4J = 76
\]

Next, we want to find the value of \( J \) by isolating it. To do this, we can divide both sides of the equation by 4:

\[
J = \frac{76}{4}
\]

Calculating the right side, we find:

\[
J = 19
\]

Now that we know Jane has $19, we can find out how much Jean has by substituting \( J \) back into the expression for Jean's money:

\[
3J = 3 \times 19
\]

Calculating this gives:

\[
3J = 57
\]

Therefore, Jean has $57.
