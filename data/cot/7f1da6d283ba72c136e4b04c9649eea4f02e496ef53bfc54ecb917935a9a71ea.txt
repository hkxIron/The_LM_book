To find out how much it costs Kim to run her TV for a week, we will follow these steps:

     \[
     125 \text{ watts} = 0.125 \text{ kilowatts} \text{ (since 1 kilowatt = 1000 watts)}
     \]
   - Now, to find out how much energy she uses in a day, we multiply the power in kilowatts by the number of hours the TV is on:
     \[
     \text{Daily energy consumption} = 0.125 \text{ kW} \times 4 \text{ hours} = 0.5 \text{ kWh}
     \]

   - Since she runs the TV for 4 hours a day, over a week (7 days), the total energy consumption will be:
     \[
     \text{Weekly energy consumption} = 0.5 \text{ kWh/day} \times 7 \text{ days} = 3.5 \text{ kWh}
     \]

3. **Calculate the cost of running the TV for a week**:
   - We know that the cost of electricity is 14 cents per kWh. To find the total cost for the week, we multiply the total energy consumption by the cost per kWh:
     \[
     \text{Cost for the week} = 3.5 \text{ kWh} \times 14 \text{ cents/kWh} = 49 \text{ cents}
     \]
