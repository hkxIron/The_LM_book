<reasoning>
Let's denote the amount of money Mark had when he entered the first store as \( x \).

1. **First Store Spending:**
   - Mark spent one-half of his money: \( \frac{1}{2}x \).
   - After this spending, he had \( x - \frac{1}{2}x = \frac{1}{2}x \) left.
   - Then he spent an additional $14: 
   \[
   \frac{1}{2}x - 14
   \]

2. **Second Store Spending:**
   - Now, he goes to the second store. At this point, he has \( \frac{1}{2}x - 14 \).
   - He spends one-third of his original money \( x \):
   \[
   \frac{1}{3}x
   \]
   - After spending this amount, he has:
   \[
   \frac{1}{2}x - 14 - \frac{1}{3}x
   \]

3. **Simplifying the Amount Left:**
   - To combine \( \frac{1}{2}x \) and \( -\frac{1}{3}x \), we need a common denominator, which is 6:
   \[
   \frac{1}{2}x = \frac{3}{6}x \quad \text{and} \quad -\frac{1}{3}x = -\frac{2}{6}x
   \]
   - Now we can combine:
   \[
   \frac{3}{6}x - \frac{2}{6}x = \frac{1}{6}x
   \]
   - Thus, the total amount Mark has after spending at the second store is:
   \[
   \frac{1}{6}x - 14
   \]

4. **Final Spending:**
   - Mark then spends $16 more:
   \[
   \frac{1}{6}x - 14 - 16 = 0
   \]
   - Simplifying this gives:
   \[
   \frac{1}{6}x - 30 = 0
   \]

5. **Solving for \( x \):**
   - To find \( x \), we add 30 to both sides:
   \[
   \frac{1}{6}x = 30
   \]
   - Now, multiply both sides by 6:
   \[
   x = 180
   \]

Thus, the amount of money Mark had when he entered the first store is \( 180 \).
