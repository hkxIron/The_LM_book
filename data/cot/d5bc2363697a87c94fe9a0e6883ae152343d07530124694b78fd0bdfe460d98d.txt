   \]

   \[
   \text{Distance} = 40 \text{ miles/hour} \times 3 \text{ hours} = 120 \text{ miles}

   \[
   \text{Remaining Distance} = 210 \text{ miles} - 120 \text{ miles} = 90 \text{ miles}
   \]
   This means Christina needs to drive 90 miles.

   We can use the distance formula again to find the time.
   \[
   \text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}}
   \]
   Here, the distance is 90 miles and the speed is 30 miles per hour.
   \[
   \text{Time} = \frac{90 \text{ miles}}{30 \text{ miles/hour}} = 3 \text{ hours}
   \]

4. **Convert the time from hours to minutes:**
   Since we need to express the time Christina drives in minutes, we convert hours to minutes. There are 60 minutes in an hour.
   \[
   \text{Time in minutes} = 3 \text{ hours} \times 60 \text{ minutes/hour} = 180 \text{ minutes}
   \]
