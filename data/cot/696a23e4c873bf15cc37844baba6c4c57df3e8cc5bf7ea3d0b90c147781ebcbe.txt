To find out how many miles Ken cycled during a week with 3 rainy days and 4 snowy days, we will first calculate how many miles he can cycle in one hour for each weather condition.

   - To find his speed in miles per hour (mph), we can first find out how many 20-minute intervals are in an hour:
     \[
     60 \text{ minutes} \div 20 \text{ minutes} = 3 \text{ intervals}
     \]
   - Therefore, in one hour, Ken can cycle:
     \[
     30 \text{ miles} \times 3 = 90 \text{ miles}
     \]

2. **Calculate Ken's cycling speed in the snow:**
   - Ken cycles 10 miles in 20 minutes.
   - Again, using the 20-minute intervals in an hour:
     \[
     60 \text{ minutes} \div 20 \text{ minutes} = 3 \text{ intervals}
     \]
   - Therefore, in one hour, Ken can cycle:
     \[
     10 \text{ miles} \times 3 = 30 \text{ miles}
     \]

3. **Calculate the total miles cycled in a week:**
   - Ken bikes for 1 hour each day. 
   - For the 3 rainy days, he cycles:
     \[
     3 \text{ days} \times 90 \text{ miles/day} = 270 \text{ miles}
     \]
   - For the 4 snowy days, he cycles:
     \[
     4 \text{ days} \times 30 \text{ miles/day} = 120 \text{ miles}
     \]

   - Now, we add the miles from the rainy days and the snowy days:
     \[
     270 \text{ miles} + 120 \text{ miles} = 390 \text{ miles}
     \]
