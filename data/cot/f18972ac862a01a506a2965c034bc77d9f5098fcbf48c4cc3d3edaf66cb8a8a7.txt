   \]

   She buys six more science books than maths books. Since she bought 4 maths books, the number of science books she buys is:
   \[
   \text{Number of science books} = 4 + 6 = 10 \text{ books}
   \]
   Each science book costs $10, so:
   \[
   \text{Cost of science books} = 10 \text{ books} \times 10 \text{ dollars/book} = 100 \text{ dollars}
   \]

   June buys twice as many art books as maths books. Since she bought 4 maths books, the number of art books is:
   \[
   \text{Number of art books} = 2 \times 4 = 8 \text{ books}
   \]

   \[
   \text{Cost of art books} = 8 \text{ books} \times 20 \text{ dollars/book} = 160 \text{ dollars}
   \]

   Now we sum up all her expenditures on maths, science, and art books:
   \[
   \text{Total spent on books} = \text{Cost of maths books} + \text{Cost of science books} + \text{Cost of art books}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Total spent on books} = 80 + 100 + 160 = 340 \text{ dollars}
   \]

   \[
   \text{Money spent on music books} = 500 \text{ dollars} - 340 \text{ dollars} = 160 \text{ dollars}
   \]
