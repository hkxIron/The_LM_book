<reasoning>
To find James's profit from a 600-mile trip, we need to calculate his total earnings from driving and subtract his total costs for gas.

**Step 1: Calculate total earnings from driving.**
James earns $0.50 per mile. For a trip of 600 miles, we calculate his total earnings as follows:

\[
\text{Total Earnings} = \text{Miles Driven} \times \text{Earnings per Mile}
\]
\[
\text{Total Earnings} = 600 \, \text{miles} \times 0.50 \, \text{dollars/mile} = 300 \, \text{dollars}
\]

**Step 2: Calculate total gas consumption for the trip.**
Next, we need to find out how many gallons of gas James's truck uses for the 600-mile trip. His truck gets 20 miles per gallon. We calculate the total gallons of gas as follows:

\[
\text{Total Gallons Used} = \frac{\text{Miles Driven}}{\text{Miles per Gallon}}
\]
\[
\text{Total Gallons Used} = \frac{600 \, \text{miles}}{20 \, \text{miles/gallon}} = 30 \, \text{gallons}
\]

**Step 3: Calculate total cost of gas.**
Now we calculate the total cost of the gas used. The cost of gas is $4.00 per gallon. Therefore, the total cost for gas is:

\[
\text{Total Cost of Gas} = \text{Total Gallons Used} \times \text{Cost per Gallon}
\]
\[
\text{Total Cost of Gas} = 30 \, \text{gallons} \times 4.00 \, \text{dollars/gallon} = 120 \, \text{dollars}
\]

**Step 4: Calculate profit.**
Finally, to find his profit, we subtract the total cost of gas from his total earnings:

\[
\text{Profit} = \text{Total Earnings} - \text{Total Cost of Gas}
\]
\[
\text{Profit} = 300 \, \text{dollars} - 120 \, \text{dollars} = 180 \, \text{dollars}
\]

Therefore, James makes a profit of $180 from the trip.
</reasoning>
<answer>180</answer>
