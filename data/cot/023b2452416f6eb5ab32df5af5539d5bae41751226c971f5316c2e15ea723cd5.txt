<reasoning>
To determine how much money Carrie will have left over after buying her bike, we first need to calculate her total earnings over the month.

1. **Calculate weekly earnings**: 
   Carrie earns $8 per hour and works 35 hours a week. 
   So, her weekly earnings can be calculated as:
   \[
   \text{Weekly Earnings} = \text{Hourly Wage} \times \text{Hours Worked} 
   \]
   \[
   \text{Weekly Earnings} = 8 \, \text{dollars/hour} \times 35 \, \text{hours} = 280 \, \text{dollars}
   \]

2. **Calculate monthly earnings**: 
   Since it has been a month since she started working, we assume she has worked 4 weeks in that month. 
   Therefore, her total earnings for the month will be:
   \[
   \text{Monthly Earnings} = \text{Weekly Earnings} \times 4 
   \]
   \[
   \text{Monthly Earnings} = 280 \, \text{dollars} \times 4 = 1120 \, \text{dollars}
   \]

3. **Calculate remaining money after buying the bike**: 
   The price of the bike is $400. To find out how much money she has left after the purchase, we subtract the cost of the bike from her total earnings:
   \[
   \text{Remaining Money} = \text{Monthly Earnings} - \text{Cost of the Bike} 
   \]
   \[
   \text{Remaining Money} = 1120 \, \text{dollars} - 400 \, \text{dollars} = 720 \, \text{dollars}
   \]

Thus, after buying the bike, Carrie will have $720 left over.
