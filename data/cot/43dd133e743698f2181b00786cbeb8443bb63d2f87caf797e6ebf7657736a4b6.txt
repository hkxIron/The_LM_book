<reasoning>
To find out how many hours Steve spends with his family in a day, we first need to determine how much of the day is taken up by the activities he participates in.

1. **Understanding the fractions of the day:**
   - Sleeping: \( \frac{1}{3} \)
   - School: \( \frac{1}{6} \)
   - Making assignments: \( \frac{1}{12} \)

2. **Finding a common denominator:**
   To combine these fractions, we need to find a common denominator. The denominators here are 3, 6, and 12. The least common multiple (LCM) of these numbers is 12.

3. **Converting each fraction:**
   - For sleeping: 
     \[
     \frac{1}{3} = \frac{4}{12} \quad \text{(because } 1 \times 4 = 4 \text{ and } 3 \times 4 = 12\text{)}
     \]
   - For school: 
     \[
     \frac{1}{6} = \frac{2}{12} \quad \text{(because } 1 \times 2 = 2 \text{ and } 6 \times 2 = 12\text{)}
     \]
   - For making assignments: 
     \[
     \frac{1}{12} = \frac{1}{12} \quad \text{(this one remains the same)}
     \]

4. **Adding the fractions of the day spent on activities:**
   Now we can add these fractions:
   \[
   \frac{4}{12} + \frac{2}{12} + \frac{1}{12} = \frac{4 + 2 + 1}{12} = \frac{7}{12}
   \]

5. **Finding the remaining fraction of the day:**
   To find out how much of the day is left for spending time with his family, we subtract the total fraction of time spent on other activities from 1 (the whole day):
   \[
   1 - \frac{7}{12} = \frac{12}{12} - \frac{7}{12} = \frac{5}{12}
   \]

6. **Calculating the hours spent with family:**
   Since there are 24 hours in a day, we multiply the fraction of the day spent with family by the total number of hours:
   \[
   \frac{5}{12} \times 24 = \frac{5 \times 24}{12} = \frac{120}{12} = 10
   \]

Therefore, Steve spends 10 hours a day with his family.
</reasoning>
