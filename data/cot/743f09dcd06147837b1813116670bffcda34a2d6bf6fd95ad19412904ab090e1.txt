<reasoning>
To solve the problem, we need to determine how many eggs Jane's chickens lay in two weeks and how much money that will generate when sold.

### Step 1: Calculate the total number of eggs produced in one week
Jane has 10 chickens, and each chicken lays 6 eggs per week. Therefore, the total number of eggs produced in one week is calculated as follows:

\[
\text{Total eggs in one week} = \text{Number of chickens} \times \text{Eggs per chicken per week}
\]

Substituting in the values:

\[
\text{Total eggs in one week} = 10 \times 6 = 60 \text{ eggs}
\]

### Step 2: Calculate the total number of eggs produced in two weeks
Now that we know the total number of eggs produced in one week, we can find out how many eggs are produced in two weeks:

\[
\text{Total eggs in two weeks} = \text{Total eggs in one week} \times 2
\]

Substituting in the value we calculated earlier:

\[
\text{Total eggs in two weeks} = 60 \times 2 = 120 \text{ eggs}
\]

### Step 3: Calculate how many dozens of eggs this is
Since Jane sells the eggs by the dozen, we need to convert the total number of eggs into dozens. 

To find out how many dozens are in 120 eggs, we use the following calculation:

\[
\text{Dozens of eggs} = \frac{\text{Total eggs}}{12}
\]

Calculating:

\[
\text{Dozens of eggs} = \frac{120}{12} = 10 \text{ dozens}
\]

### Step 4: Calculate the total sales from selling the eggs
Jane sells the eggs for $2 per dozen. To find out how much money she makes from selling 10 dozens, we calculate:

\[
\text{Total sales} = \text{Dozens of eggs} \times \text{Price per dozen}
\]

Substituting in the values:

\[
\text{Total sales} = 10 \times 2 = 20
\]

### Conclusion
Thus, Jane will make a total of $20 from selling all her eggs over the course of two weeks.
