Let's denote the cost of jellyfish as \( x \). 

1. Since the eel costs nine times as much as the jellyfish, we can express the cost of eel as \( 9x \).
2. According to the problem, the combined cost of one order of jellyfish and one order of eel is $200. Therefore, we can set up the following equation:
   \[
   x + 9x = 200
   \]
   Here, \( x \) is the cost of the jellyfish and \( 9x \) is the cost of the eel.

3. Now, we simplify the left side of the equation:
   \[
   10x = 200
   \]
   This is because \( x + 9x \) simplifies to \( 10x \).

4. Next, we solve for \( x \) by dividing both sides of the equation by 10:
   \[
   x = \frac{200}{10} = 20
   \]
   Thus, the cost of the jellyfish is $20.

5. Now, we need to find the cost of the eel. Since the eel costs nine times the amount of the jellyfish, we calculate:
   \[
   9x = 9 \times 20 = 180
   \]
   Therefore, the cost of the eel is $180.
