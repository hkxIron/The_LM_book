<reasoning>
Let's denote the initial amount of money Susan had as \( x \).

1. In September, Susan spent \( \frac{1}{5} \) of her total amount:
   \[
   \text{Amount spent in September} = \frac{1}{5} x
   \]

2. In October, she spent \( \frac{1}{4} \) of her total amount:
   \[
   \text{Amount spent in October} = \frac{1}{4} x
   \]

3. In November, she spent a fixed amount of $120:
   \[
   \text{Amount spent in November} = 120
   \]

4. After these expenditures, she had $540 left. We can now set up the equation to represent the total money after her expenses:
   \[
   x - \left(\frac{1}{5} x + \frac{1}{4} x + 120\right) = 540
   \]

5. To simplify, combine the fractions \( \frac{1}{5} x \) and \( \frac{1}{4} x \). To do this, we need a common denominator, which is 20:
   \[
   \frac{1}{5} x = \frac{4}{20} x \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{1}{4} x = \frac{5}{20} x
   \]
   So,
   \[
   \frac{1}{5} x + \frac{1}{4} x = \frac{4}{20} x + \frac{5}{20} x = \frac{9}{20} x
   \]

6. We substitute this back into our equation:
   \[
   x - \left(\frac{9}{20} x + 120\right) = 540
   \]

7. Distributing the negative sign:
   \[
   x - \frac{9}{20} x - 120 = 540
   \]

8. Combine the \( x \) terms:
   \[
   \left(1 - \frac{9}{20}\right)x - 120 = 540
   \]
   The coefficient of \( x \) becomes:
   \[
   1 - \frac{9}{20} = \frac{20}{20} - \frac{9}{20} = \frac{11}{20}
   \]
   Thus, we have:
   \[
   \frac{11}{20} x - 120 = 540
   \]

9. Now, add 120 to both sides:
   \[
   \frac{11}{20} x = 540 + 120
   \]
   \[
   \frac{11}{20} x = 660
   \]

10. To solve for \( x \), multiply both sides by the reciprocal of \( \frac{11}{20} \):
    \[
    x = 660 \times \frac{20}{11}
    \]

11. Calculating \( 660 \times \frac{20}{11} \):
    - First, calculate \( 660 \times 20 = 13200 \)
    - Now divide by 11:
    \[
    \frac{13200}{11} = 1200
    \]

Therefore, Susan initially had $1200.
</reasoning>
