   We know that 10 hens lay 80 eggs in 10 days. To find the daily egg-laying rate, we need to find out how many eggs they lay in one day.
   
   \[
   \text{Eggs per day by 10 hens} = \frac{80 \text{ eggs}}{10 \text{ days}} = 8 \text{ eggs per day}
   \]
   
   This means 10 hens produce 8 eggs per day.

   To find out how many eggs one hen lays per day, we divide the total number of eggs by the number of hens.
   
   \[
   \text{Eggs per hen per day} = \frac{8 \text{ eggs}}{10 \text{ hens}} = 0.8 \text{ eggs per hen per day}
   \]

3. **Calculate the total number of hens after purchasing more:**
   Martin originally has 10 hens and buys 15 more hens. Therefore, the total number of hens is:
   
   \[
   \text{Total hens} = 10 + 15 = 25 \text{ hens}
   \]

4. **Calculate the total egg-laying capacity of all hens in one day:**
   Now that we know each hen lays 0.8 eggs per day, we can find out how many eggs 25 hens will lay in one day.
   
   \[
   \text{Eggs per day by 25 hens} = 25 \text{ hens} \times 0.8 \text{ eggs per hen} = 20 \text{ eggs per day}
   \]

5. **Calculate the total eggs laid in 15 days:**
   To find out how many eggs are laid in 15 days, we multiply the daily output by the number of days.
   
   \[
   \text{Total eggs in 15 days} = 20 \text{ eggs per day} \times 15 \text{ days} = 300 \text{ eggs}
   \]
