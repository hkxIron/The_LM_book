<reasoning>
Let's start by determining how many cookies each person baked.

1. **Clementine's cookies**: 
   - We know that Clementine baked 72 cookies.

2. **Jake's cookies**: 
   - Jake baked twice as many cookies as Clementine. 
   - So, we calculate Jake's cookies: 
   \[
   \text{Jake's cookies} = 2 \times \text{Clementine's cookies} = 2 \times 72 = 144
   \]

3. **Tory's cookies**: 
   - Tory baked half as many cookies as Jake and Clementine combined.
   - First, we need to find the total number of cookies that Clementine and Jake baked together:
   \[
   \text{Total cookies by Clementine and Jake} = \text{Clementine's cookies} + \text{Jake's cookies} = 72 + 144 = 216
   \]
   - Now, we calculate Tory's cookies:
   \[
   \text{Tory's cookies} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Total cookies by Clementine and Jake} = \frac{1}{2} \times 216 = 108
   \]

4. **Total cookies made**: 
   - Now, we find the total number of cookies made by all three:
   \[
   \text{Total cookies} = \text{Clementine's cookies} + \text{Jake's cookies} + \text{Tory's cookies} = 72 + 144 + 108
   \]
   - Calculating this gives:
   \[
   \text{Total cookies} = 72 + 144 = 216
   \]
   \[
   216 + 108 = 324
   \]

5. **Calculating total sales**: 
   - They sell their cookies for $2 each, so we multiply the total number of cookies by the price per cookie:
   \[
   \text{Total money made} = \text{Total cookies} \times \text{Price per cookie} = 324 \times 2
   \]
   - Performing the multiplication:
   \[
   324 \times 2 = 648
   \]

Therefore, the total money made from selling all the cookies is $648.
