<reasoning>
To find the total number of passengers transported between the two stations, we start by calculating the number of passengers for each trip.

1. **First Trip (One Way)**: The train carried 100 passengers from one station to the other. 
   
   - **Passengers transported in this trip**: 100

2. **Return Trip**: On the return journey, the train carried 60 passengers back to the original station.

   - **Passengers transported in this trip**: 60

Now, we will calculate the total passengers for this round trip (to and from):

- **Total for the first round trip**: 
  \[
  100 \text{ (to)} + 60 \text{ (return)} = 160
  \]

3. **Additional Round Trips**: The train made three more round trips, carrying the same number of passengers as the first trip (100 to the destination and 60 back).

   - **Total for each additional round trip**: 160 (as calculated above)

   - **Total for three additional round trips**:
   \[
   3 \text{ (round trips)} \times 160 \text{ (passengers per round trip)} = 480
   \]

4. **Total Passengers for All Trips**: Now, we add the passengers from the initial round trip to the passengers from the three additional round trips.

   - **Total passengers transported**:
   \[
   160 \text{ (first round trip)} + 480 \text{ (three additional round trips)} = 640
   \]

Therefore, the total number of passengers transported between both stations is 640.
