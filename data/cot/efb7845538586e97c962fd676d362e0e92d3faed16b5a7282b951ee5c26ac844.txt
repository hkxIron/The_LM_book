<reasoning>
Let's denote Sara's height as \( S \) inches and Joe's height as \( J \) inches. We are given two pieces of information:

1. Their combined height is 120 inches:
   \[
   S + J = 120
   \]

2. Joe is 6 inches more than double Sara's height:
   \[
   J = 2S + 6
   \]

Now, we will substitute the expression for \( J \) from the second equation into the first equation. This allows us to express everything in terms of \( S \).

Substituting \( J \) into the first equation:
\[
S + (2S + 6) = 120
\]

Now, we will combine like terms:
\[
3S + 6 = 120
\]

Next, we will isolate \( 3S \) by subtracting 6 from both sides:
\[
3S = 120 - 6
\]
\[
3S = 114
\]

Now, we will solve for \( S \) by dividing both sides by 3:
\[
S = \frac{114}{3} = 38
\]

Now that we have Sara's height, we can find Joe's height using the second equation:
\[
J = 2S + 6
\]
Substituting \( S = 38 \):
\[
J = 2(38) + 6
\]
Calculating this gives:
\[
J = 76 + 6 = 82
\]

Thus, Joe's height is 82 inches.
</reasoning>
