<reasoning>
To find out how many plants Shyne can grow in her backyard, we need to calculate the total number of eggplants and sunflowers she can grow based on the seed packets she bought.

1. **Calculate the number of eggplants**:
   - We know that each seed packet of eggplants allows Shyne to grow 14 eggplants.
   - She bought 4 seed packets of eggplants.
   - So, we multiply the number of eggplants per packet by the number of packets:
     \[
     \text{Total eggplants} = 14 \, \text{eggplants/packet} \times 4 \, \text{packets} 
     \]
   - Performing the multiplication:
     \[
     \text{Total eggplants} = 56 \, \text{eggplants}
     \]

2. **Calculate the number of sunflowers**:
   - Each seed packet of sunflowers allows her to grow 10 sunflowers.
   - She bought 6 seed packets of sunflowers.
   - Similarly, we multiply the number of sunflowers per packet by the number of packets:
     \[
     \text{Total sunflowers} = 10 \, \text{sunflowers/packet} \times 6 \, \text{packets}
     \]
   - Performing this multiplication:
     \[
     \text{Total sunflowers} = 60 \, \text{sunflowers}
     \]

3. **Calculate the total number of plants**:
   - Now, we need to find the total number of plants by adding the total eggplants and total sunflowers:
     \[
     \text{Total plants} = \text{Total eggplants} + \text{Total sunflowers}
     \]
   - Substituting the values we found:
     \[
     \text{Total plants} = 56 \, \text{eggplants} + 60 \, \text{sunflowers}
     \]
   - Performing the addition:
     \[
     \text{Total plants} = 116
     \]

Therefore, Shyne can grow a total of 116 plants in her backyard.
