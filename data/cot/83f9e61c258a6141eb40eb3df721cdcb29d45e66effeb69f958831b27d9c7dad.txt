   \[
   \text{Number of cakes} = \frac{\text{Flour for cakes}}{\text{Flour per cake}} = \frac{4 \text{ pounds}}{0.5 \text{ pounds per cake}} = 8 \text{ cakes}
   \]
   Here, we divide the total flour for cakes (4 pounds) by the amount of flour required for each cake (0.5 pounds) to find out how many cakes Gary can make.

   \[
   \text{Total earnings from cakes} = \text{Number of cakes} \times \text{Price per cake} = 8 \text{ cakes} \times 2.5 \text{ dollars per cake} = 20 \text{ dollars}
   \]
   We multiply the number of cakes (8) by the selling price of each cake ($2.5) to find the total earnings from the cakes.

3. Now, let's calculate how many cupcakes Gary can make with the remaining 2 pounds of flour. Each cupcake requires \(\frac{1}{5}\) pounds of flour.
   \[
   \text{Number of cupcakes} = \frac{\text{Flour for cupcakes}}{\text{Flour per cupcake}} = \frac{2 \text{ pounds}}{\frac{1}{5} \text{ pounds per cupcake}} = 2 \times 5 = 10 \text{ cupcakes}
   \]
   To find the number of cupcakes, we divide the total flour for cupcakes (2 pounds) by the amount of flour required for each cupcake (\(\frac{1}{5}\) pounds). This is equivalent to multiplying by the reciprocal of \(\frac{1}{5}\), which is 5.

4. Lastly, we calculate how much Gary earns from selling these cupcakes. He plans to sell each cupcake for $1.
   \[
   \text{Total earnings from cupcakes} = \text{Number of cupcakes} \times \text{Price per cupcake} = 10 \text{ cupcakes} \times 1 \text{ dollar per cupcake} = 10 \text{ dollars}
   \]
   Here, we multiply the number of cupcakes (10) by the selling price of each cupcake ($1) to find the total earnings from the cupcakes.

5. Finally, we sum up the total earnings from both cakes and cupcakes to find Gary's total earnings:
   \[
   \text{Total earnings} = \text{Earnings from cakes} + \text{Earnings from cupcakes} = 20 \text{ dollars} + 10 \text{ dollars} = 30 \text{ dollars}
   \]

Therefore, Gary will earn a total of $30 from the bake sale.
