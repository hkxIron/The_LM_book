<reasoning>
First, we need to determine how many hens Uncle Ben has. We know that he has a total of 440 chickens and that 39 of them are roosters. 

To find the number of hens, we subtract the number of roosters from the total number of chickens:

\[ 
\text{Number of hens} = \text{Total chickens} - \text{Number of roosters} 
\]
\[ 
\text{Number of hens} = 440 - 39 
\]
\[ 
\text{Number of hens} = 401 
\]

So, Uncle Ben has 401 hens.

Next, we need to find out how many of these hens lay eggs. We know that 15 of the hens do not lay eggs. Therefore, we subtract the non-egg-laying hens from the total number of hens:

\[ 
\text{Number of egg-laying hens} = \text{Total hens} - \text{Non-egg-laying hens} 
\]
\[ 
\text{Number of egg-laying hens} = 401 - 15 
\]
\[ 
\text{Number of egg-laying hens} = 386 
\]

Now, we know that each egg-laying hen lays 3 eggs. To find the total number of eggs laid by all egg-laying hens, we multiply the number of egg-laying hens by the number of eggs each lays:

\[ 
\text{Total eggs} = \text{Number of egg-laying hens} \times \text{Eggs per hen} 
\]
\[ 
\text{Total eggs} = 386 \times 3 
\]
\[ 
\text{Total eggs} = 1158 
\]

Therefore, Uncle Ben will have a total of 1158 eggs from his egg-laying hens.
