<reasoning>
First, let's calculate how much money Stormi makes from washing cars. She washes 3 cars for $10 each.

1. **Calculating earnings from washing cars:**
   \[
   \text{Earnings from cars} = \text{Number of cars} \times \text{Price per car}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Earnings from cars} = 3 \times 10 = 30
   \]

Now, Stormi has made $30 from washing cars.

2. **Next, let's calculate how much she makes from mowing lawns. She mows 2 lawns for $13 each.**
   \[
   \text{Earnings from lawns} = \text{Number of lawns} \times \text{Price per lawn}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Earnings from lawns} = 2 \times 13 = 26
   \]

Now, Stormi has made an additional $26 from mowing lawns.

3. **Now, let's find the total amount of money Stormi has made.**
   \[
   \text{Total earnings} = \text{Earnings from cars} + \text{Earnings from lawns}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Total earnings} = 30 + 26 = 56
   \]

So far, Stormi has a total of $56.

4. **Next, we need to find out how much more money she needs to buy the bicycle, which costs $80.**
   \[
   \text{Amount needed} = \text{Cost of bicycle} - \text{Total earnings}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Amount needed} = 80 - 56 = 24
   \]

Therefore, Stormi needs to make $24 more to afford the bicycle.
