<reasoning>
Let's define the ages of Markus, his son, and his grandson with variables:

- Let \( M \) be Markus's age.
- Let \( S \) be Markus's son's age.
- Let \( G \) be Markus's grandson's age.

According to the problem, we have the following relationships:

1. Markus is twice the age of his son:
   \[
   M = 2S
   \]

2. Markus's son is twice the age of Markus's grandson:
   \[
   S = 2G
   \]

3. The sum of their ages is 140 years:
   \[
   M + S + G = 140
   \]

Now, we can substitute the expressions for \( M \) and \( S \) from the first two equations into the sum equation.

Starting with the sum equation:
\[
M + S + G = 140
\]

Substituting \( M = 2S \) into the equation:
\[
2S + S + G = 140
\]

This simplifies to:
\[
3S + G = 140
\]

Now, substitute \( S = 2G \) into the equation:
\[
3(2G) + G = 140
\]

This simplifies to:
\[
6G + G = 140
\]
\[
7G = 140
\]

Now, divide both sides by 7 to solve for \( G \):
\[
G = \frac{140}{7} = 20
\]

Thus, Markus's grandson is 20 years old. 

Therefore, we conclude that:
</reasoning>
