   - Current distance: 2 miles
   - Target distance: 20 miles

   - We subtract his current distance from the target distance:
     \[
     \text{Distance to cover} = \text{Target distance} - \text{Current distance} = 20 - 2 = 18 \text{ miles}
     \]

3. **Determine the weekly distance increase**:
   - Oscar plans to add 2/3 of a mile each week.

4. **Calculate how many weeks it will take to cover the remaining distance**:
   - We need to divide the total distance he needs to cover by the distance he increases each week:
     \[
     \text{Weeks needed} = \frac{\text{Distance to cover}}{\text{Weekly increase}} = \frac{18}{\frac{2}{3}}
     \]
   - To divide by a fraction, we multiply by its reciprocal:
     \[
     \text{Weeks needed} = 18 \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{18 \times 3}{2} = \frac{54}{2} = 27
     \]

5. **Conclusion**:
   - Oscar needs to train for 27 weeks before the marathon to reach his goal of running 20 miles.

</reasoning>
